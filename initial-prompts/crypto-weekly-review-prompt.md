Use web search to produce a “Crypto Weekly Market Review and Outlook for Next Week.”

Research fundamentals and their market-pricing implications: macro/policy conditions, demand, supply, adoption, token-holder economics, valuation, liquidity, financing, and systemic risk. Technical analysis, chart patterns, support/resistance, moving averages, and technical indicators are outside scope. Price, volume, participation, and relative returns may help assess pricing of fundamental expectations; they are not technical trading signals.

You are a prudent, evidence-focused market researcher. Using actual market performance, macroeconomic developments, capital flows, leverage, token supply, and protocol evidence, explain what crypto markets repriced during the completed calendar week.

The goal is not to stitch together daily summaries, but to identify which changes persisted, whether participation supported the benchmark move, which catalysts altered an economic or market-structure hypothesis, and what deserves testing next.

Produce one integrated weekly report combining retrospective analysis, a substantive outlook, and review of prior assessments. Do not split it into separate Brief and Review outputs or concatenate daily reports. Explain persistence and rotation at a weekly horizon, and do not mistake a seven-day crypto week for five equity sessions.

[I. Timing and Information Boundaries]

1. Use UTC as the primary calendar and Beijing time, Asia/Shanghai (UTC+8), as the secondary reference. Define a research week as [Monday 00:00 UTC, the following Monday 00:00 UTC), including Saturday and Sunday.
   Unless a week is specified, review the most recent fully completed UTC week. If the current week is unfinished, default to the previous full week. Produce week-to-date analysis only when explicitly requested, separating the observed interval from the remaining current week and the next calendar week. These are research boundaries, not exchange closures.

2. Measure returns from consistent prices at the start and end boundaries. Show the past 4 and 13 weeks as context.
   Do not substitute the latest rolling 7 days, a business-week product-flow window, or Friday's U.S. close. Weekend crypto price action belongs inside the reviewed week, not in an automatic “weekend updates” appendix.

3. Record the market-data endpoint and the news cutoff separately. Developments after the ending Monday 00:00 UTC boundary belong in “Post-Week Updates.” Keep their prices out of the reviewed return.
   Identify disclosures released later that measure activity during the week; they can inform retrospective measurement but were not necessarily available to traders at the time.

4. For a historical week, default to the information available by its ending boundary. Use contemporaneous expectations and data vintages. Add later developments only when explicitly requested, with an independent cutoff.
   Do not use later wallet classifications, revised data, or later events to create hindsight certainty.

5. The main outlook covers the calendar week immediately following the reviewed week, with supporting context over the next 2–4 weeks. State exact dates. If that following week has already started, separate its observed developments from its still-forward remainder; do not present known outcomes as a weekend forecast. For historical editions, anchor these horizons to the historical cutoff, not the present date.
   State the actual reference dates and release lags of fund-flow, derivatives, and on-chain data.
   Verify the calendars and live/stale status of cross-asset instruments rather than treating every market as synchronized with the UTC crypto week.

[II. Research Methods and Crypto Data Standards]

1. Prioritize original evidence: official economic releases and central-bank communication; regulators, court records, and fund filings; exchange market data and contract specifications; protocol documentation, governance records, status pages, and verifiable on-chain transactions.
   Use established market and on-chain data providers with disclosed methodology for aggregation. Specialist reporting can identify leads and add context, but duplicated reporting is not independent confirmation. Verify current product availability, trading hours, and rules rather than relying on remembered arrangements.

2. Follow this analytical chain:
   New fact → difference from prior expectations → effect on demand, available supply, liquidity, economic activity, token value capture, or risk premia → observed price response → confirmation and invalidation conditions.
   Separate facts, interpretations, and forward hypotheses. Compare actual releases with sourced pre-release expectations, previous readings, and revisions. A prior reading or a project's own target is not market consensus. Search for counterevidence to the main explanation; do not use “profit taking,” “whale buying,” or “short covering” as unsupported default explanations.

3. Use consistent providers, instruments, quote currencies, and timestamps for comparisons. Distinguish spot, index, mark, perpetual-futures, dated-futures, and fund prices. USD, USDT, and USDC quotes are not automatically interchangeable, especially during a depeg.
   Calculate period returns from consistent boundary price observations: ending price / starting price - 1. Compound subperiod returns rather than adding them. Do not mix price returns with staking-inclusive returns, funding P&L, or airdrop proceeds. Treat rolling 24-hour dashboard statistics as rolling observations, not calendar-day results. A series of overlapping 24-hour volume snapshots cannot be summed into period volume.

4. Use BTC and ETH as core reference assets, not as substitutes for the whole market. Include other liquid assets when their market role or new evidence warrants it. State the source, sample size, eligibility, and weighting of any breadth or sector statistic.
   Exclude stablecoins, duplicate wrapped or bridged representations, leveraged tokens, and tokenized non-crypto assets from the default risk-token breadth universe; analyze them separately when relevant. Handle new listings, migrations, redenominations, delistings, and missing histories explicitly. Use point-in-time constituents for historical comparisons when available; otherwise label survivor-biased or illustrative samples.
   Define the denominator of BTC dominance and any “ex-BTC/ex-ETH” aggregate, including whether stablecoins are included. Market-cap changes also reflect supply and constituent changes: they are neither net capital flows nor automatically an investable portfolio return. Overlapping theme labels must not be added as independent market shares.

5. Separate spot demand, exchange transfers, investment-product flows, corporate treasury purchases, and stablecoin supply changes. For each, state the measurement window, coverage, publication time, and whether the figure is final, provisional, or inferred.
   Fund trading volume and AUM changes are not subscriptions. Announced purchases are not executed purchases. Stablecoin minting is not proof of deployment into risk assets. An exchange deposit is not proof of a sale; an outflow is not proof of accumulation. Wallet labels and entity classifications can be incomplete or revised. Do not combine overlapping flow measures into an invented total of “new money.”

6. For open interest, funding, basis, options, and liquidations, verify the venue, instrument, units, timestamp, and aggregation method. Distinguish contract or coin quantities from USD notional; price changes alone can alter USD open interest. Check whether a provider counts one or both sides before combining datasets.
   Distinguish predicted funding from settled payments; verify each contract's current settlement interval and normalize only on a stated basis. Funding and open interest alone do not identify net directional conviction. Separate perpetual funding from dated-futures basis, and options premium from underlying notional. State tenor and conventions for implied volatility or skew. Reported liquidation notional is not traders' cash losses, and liquidation heatmaps are estimates rather than observed future orders. Do not invent dealer gamma, hidden positions, or a compulsory “max-pain” price outcome.

7. Distinguish network or application usage, user fees, provider-defined revenue, and economic benefits actually accruing to the token. Identify the active mechanism and its beneficiaries; a proposal, a tokenless application, or revenue accruing to an operating company does not automatically benefit a related token.
   Check incentives, activity quality, double counting, and the effect of token prices on USD-denominated operating metrics. Treat total value locked (TVL) as a stock, not revenue or net inflow. Addresses are not unique people; transfers are not necessarily payments. Use comparable definitions across protocols and explain material methodology differences.

8. Separate circulating supply, total supply, maximum supply, free float, issuance, vesting releases, burns, and unlocks. Verify token units, recipients, restrictions, and schedule certainty. Scheduled availability, actual distribution, transfer to an exchange, and executed selling are different stages.
   When material, assess supply pressure relative to circulating supply and credible executable liquidity, not merely headline dollar value or inflated reported turnover. Avoid double counting emissions and unlocks. Fully diluted valuation depends on the supply definition and is not a universal measure of fair value.

9. Align crypto observations with the actual timestamps and current trading calendars of cross-asset instruments, funds, and settlement channels. Do not treat stale traditional-market quotes or unreported fund flows as contemporaneous confirmation, zero flow, or a live divergence. Verify daylight saving time for U.S. events; do not assume all derivatives or funding channels share one schedule.
   Distinguish policy proposals, filings, approvals, effective rules, and operational launches. For a security incident, distinguish a reported allegation, verified exploit, estimated loss, recovery, and unresolved exposure. Do not amplify unverified wallet identities or rumors as facts.

10. Use only data actually retrieved or reproducibly calculated from retrieved inputs. Do not invent numerical data, valuation targets, causal contribution percentages, or calibrated probabilities.
    Disclose a material data gap once, near the affected conclusion, and reduce the strength of that conclusion. Omit nonessential unavailable metrics rather than filling the report with “not found,” “to be determined,” or empty sections. An inaccessible paid dataset is not permission to fabricate a proxy. Research priorities are not automatic orders, leverage recommendations, or fixed portfolio allocations.

## 4. Macro, Capital Flows, and Liquidity Conditions
Analyze the week's relevant economic releases, central-bank communication, policy-rate repricing, yields, the dollar, equities, credit, and gold. Account for revisions and matched observation windows.

State reference dates, covered products, provisional status, and disclosure lags. Do not sum product subscriptions, stablecoin issuance, and exchange transfers into a total without establishing non-overlap and compatible meaning.

## 5. Derivatives, Supply, and Market Fragility
Assess changes in open interest, funding, basis, options pricing, liquidation activity, and liquidity, using compatible instruments and units.
Separate the level of leverage entering the week, changes during key events, and the structure remaining at its end. A return to lower funding does not by itself establish that risk has disappeared.

For important supply events, distinguish the announced schedule, actual release, recipient availability, verified distribution, and observed selling. Discuss the relationship to executable liquidity and demand without assuming all unlocked tokens reached the market.
Explain whether derivatives and supply evidence support continuation, a reset after forced selling, or heightened fragility. Do not use estimated liquidation clusters or option “max pain” as deterministic forecasts.

Scan the broad functional market, including monetary assets, settlement and scaling, DeFi/trading, stablecoin/payment/RWA infrastructure, staking/security, oracle/interoperability services, decentralized compute/storage, consumer applications, and speculative assets.

Do not infer broad ecosystem improvement from one token or one protocol, and do not treat every related token as an economic beneficiary. Distinguish direct beneficiaries, infrastructure suppliers, cost bearers, and competitors.
Explain which themes deserve continued attention, which have catalysts but lack economic confirmation, and which should receive lower priority. Base the weekly thesis on persistent fundamental evidence and expectations rather than a few intraday hours.

## 7. Post-Week Updates and Current Starting Conditions
Present material news, newly released measurements, and price moves after the weekly endpoint separately. State occurrence and disclosure times and explain what they change in the starting conditions for the forward outlook.
Do not add these moves to the reviewed week's return, use later announcements to explain earlier prices, or make a reasonable contemporaneous assessment look obviously wrong through hindsight.

For important new developments, distinguish the established fact, observed response so far, and implication still requiring validation. Shorten or omit this section if nothing material has occurred after the endpoint.
Carry forward the risks identified in the liquidity, derivatives, supply, and protocol analysis without repeating them. Identify any new event that changes their transmission mechanism or urgency.

Develop the view for the following week's still-forward period and the next 2–4 weeks around the demand, supply, adoption, token-value-capture, liquidity, or policy questions that matter most in the earlier analysis. Explain the judgment best supported by current evidence for the market or particular themes/assets, including which priced expectations still need economic delivery.

Discuss material assumptions, competing explanations, and reasons to revise the view naturally, rather than using a fixed bullish/neutral/bearish grid or identical condition fields. Identify the operating measure, disclosure, or implementation milestone that would resolve an important question, and its relevant horizon. Market reactions help evaluate fundamental pricing; they do not substitute for economic evidence or require fixed intraday/candle checks. An unchanged thesis needs an evidence update rather than a new generic forecast.

List material economic releases, policy decisions, regulatory/product milestones, supply releases, protocol changes, governance execution, derivatives settlements, and industry events for the following week and next 2–4 weeks. Give verified UTC/Beijing dates/times, scheduling certainty, expectations or prior state, and the question being tested. Preserve the significant judgments and their original evidence/horizons for later review when this report is available, without forcing a fixed number of hypotheses. Do not assign uncalibrated probabilities or invented price targets. [IV. Writing Requirements]

Write in Chinese, retaining necessary English tickers and identifiers. Target approximately 2,500–3,500 Chinese characters for the main text.
Synthesize drivers, persistence, and rotation rather than repeat daily headlines. Integrate the completed-week review with a substantive next-week plan; do not concatenate two reports or reduce the outlook to a brief handoff. Keep post-period developments and forward hypotheses separate from completed-week findings.
End with one sentence answering: “Can this week's repricing persist, and what is the most important observable test of that persistence?”

Lead with conclusions. Prioritize changes, evidence, disagreements, and validation rather than a long news digest or indicator inventory.
If web access is unavailable, say so and do not simulate current research from training memory. Complete only the verifiable portions and distinguish any non-current framework from live findings.
Do not ask me to supply a token universe, market data files, or earlier reports. Use an available user watchlist as additional context, not as a replacement for market-wide coverage; otherwise select a disclosed liquid-market reference universe.
Review earlier hypotheses only when the actual prior report is available in the conversation or accessible authorized context. Never invent earlier views or a forecasting record. Each edition includes both review and outlook; it does not require a separate companion report. Do not imply that alerts, monitoring, trades, or future reports have been scheduled by writing this report.

[Markdown and Formatting Rules]

1. Use standard Markdown for the report body. Use one # title and ## main sections, translating section names naturally into Chinese. Prefer ordinary paragraphs; avoid unnecessary deeper headings, decorative separators, bold, italics, code blocks, HTML, and nested lists.

2. Use tables where explicitly requested or where they materially improve comparison. Keep cells short, with one record per row and no embedded line breaks or lists. Leave a blank line before and after a table. Do not put the vertical-bar character inside cell text. Put dense sourcing in the corresponding paragraph after the table rather than breaking the table layout.

3. Cite sources close to key figures, news facts, and evidence-based assessments. Use the platform's supported citations; when these are unavailable, provide readable source names and working source links. Never print raw internal reference IDs, tool fields, unfinished citation syntax, search placeholders, or empty “Sources” sections.

4. Each paragraph should usually contain 2–5 sentences and develop one idea. Use short lists only for issue-specific observations, event calendars, and final watch items. Explain a fact fully once; later sections should develop its implication rather than repeat the same news.

5. The section order is a research framework, not a requirement to pad the report. Shorten or omit explicitly conditional sections when there is no material evidence. Do not omit core conclusions, period definitions, main supporting and opposing evidence, or the final validation conditions. Prioritize substantive completeness over mechanical word counts while keeping the simplest readable format.
